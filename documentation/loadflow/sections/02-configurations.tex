\section{Configurations covered}
\label{sec:configurations}

\subsection{Symmetrical monopole}
The symmetrical monopole carries the full power on two conductors held at
$+U_{dpe}$ and $-U_{dpe}$ against ground, with no third conductor and no ground
path in service. The DC circuit is a single loop: current leaves the rectifier
on the positive conductor and returns on the negative one. The voltage seen by
the converter is therefore the pole-to-pole voltage
\[
  U_{dpp} = 2\cdot U_{dpe},
\]
and the transmitted power is
\[
  P_{dc} = U_{dpp}\cdot I_{d}.
\]

\subsection{Bipole with dedicated metallic return}
The bipole with dedicated return runs two independently controlled poles at $\pm U_{dpe}$ plus a third,
lower-rated conductor: the dedicated metallic return. In normal
\textbf{balanced} operation the two pole currents are equal and opposite, the
DMR carries no current, and the circuit is electrically identical to the
symmetrical monopole:
\[
  P_{dc} = 2\cdot U_{dpe}\cdot I_{d},
  \qquad
  I_{d\_dmr} = 0 .
\]
The DMR earns its place when the bipole becomes unbalanced. With one pole out
of service the remaining pole keeps operating and its current returns through
the DMR instead of the lost conductor, so the link continues at reduced
capacity. In that condition the circuit is a single-pole loop:
\[
  P_{dc} = U_{dpe}\cdot I_{d},
\]
and the return path resistance is that of the DMR rather than that of a second
HV bundle --- which is why the DMR conductor is a separate input in the
calculation, as it is generally not the same conductor as the poles.

\subsection{Reduced-capacity operating state}
With one pole out of service the link is a different circuit, and it is treated
as its own operating state rather than as a fraction of the healthy one: a
post-contingency transfer is set at the receiving end and the current that
delivers it through one pole and the DMR follows. The plant is unchanged ---
same voltage, length, conductors, station losses and conductor temperature ---
so only the power and the return path distinguish the state from balanced
operation.

Two consequences are worth stating. The pole current in this state is
\emph{not} half the bipolar current: at half the power on one pole instead of
two, the current is of the same order as the full bipolar value, and with the
more resistive DMR in the return path it comes out slightly above it. It is
therefore the outage state, not normal operation, that can set the conductor
and valve rating. And because the same order of current flows through a loop of
higher resistance while carrying half the power, the loss as a fraction of the
transfer is several times worse than in balanced operation.
