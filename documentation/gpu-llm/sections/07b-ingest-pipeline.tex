\section{The ingest pipeline in detail}
\label{sec:ingest-pipeline}

Ingest is the part of \textit{corpus} that has needed the most hardening, and
for one reason: it runs heavy, unpredictable, per-document work over thousands
of heterogeneous PDFs on a machine with 7\,GB of RAM. Retrieval and answering
are well-behaved by comparison. This section documents the pipeline as it now
stands, including every repair and every limit, because each one exists in
response to a specific failure rather than as a precaution.

\subsection{Character repair}

Four distinct corruptions arrive from PDF extraction. None announces itself:
the text looks plausible, and only a search that should match and does not
reveals the damage.

\begin{table}[H]
  \centering\small
  \begin{tabular}{@{}llp{62mm}@{}}
    \toprule
    Damage & Appears as & Repair \\
    \midrule
    Symbol-font glyphs & \cmd{U+F020--F0FF} &
      Adobe Symbol encoding table. Recovers
      $\leq\ \geq\ \Delta\ \omega\ \partial\ \alpha\ \pi\ \mu\ \Omega\ \sum$.
      Affected 101 of 248 documents when first measured; 3\,747 characters. \\
    T1 ligatures & ``conrm'', ``lesystem'' &
      Translate surviving control characters, then repair dropped ones from the
      document's own vocabulary. Ambiguous cases are left alone. \\
    UTF-16 surrogates & \cmd{U+D800--DFFF} &
      Re-pair valid high/low pairs; drop orphans. A lone surrogate cannot be
      encoded as UTF-8 and crashed the writer outright. \\
    Maths alphanumerics & $\mathit{V}$ vs \cmd{V} &
      NFKC folding, applied only to \cmd{U+1D400--1D7FF}, so a variable set in
      mathematical italic matches the same letter in running text. \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \caption{Character-level repairs, all applied during \cmd{tidy()}.}

\end{table}

\subsection{Why ingest is bounded}

A single process walking thousands of PDFs is killed by the kernel long before
it finishes. The cost of parsing does not correlate with anything visible in
advance: a 1.7\,MB conference poster carries 83\,645 drawing operations on an
A0 page and wants more memory than a 135-page brochure. Five such runs were
OOM-killed before the controls below were in place.

\subsection{Running it}

\begin{lstlisting}[basicstyle=\ttfamily\small]
./ingest-all 400          # batches of 400, restart until done
./rag ingest --batch 400  # one batch; exit 3 if more remain
./rag rechunk             # re-chunk and re-embed from md/, no PDF re-parsing
\end{lstlisting}

\noindent
Progress is durable at every level. Parsed markdown is cached on the source's
sha256, each document's chunks are written as they are embedded, and search
indexes are built only on the final batch. A run that dies loses the batch in
flight and nothing else --- which is why the corpus grew from 1\,967 to over
5\,800 documents across five OOM kills and two deadlocks.
