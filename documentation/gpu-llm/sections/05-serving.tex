\section{Fetching and serving the model}
\label{sec:serving}

\subsection{Start the server}
Keep this in a script on \cmd{/shared}. Long quoted one-liners do not survive
the relay's re-quoting.

\begin{lstlisting}
#!/bin/bash
# /shared/$USER/start-ollama.sh   --  run: gpu shell /shared/$USER/start-ollama.sh
set -u
BIN=/shared/$USER/opt/ollama/bin/ollama
LOG=/shared/$USER/ollama-serve.log

export OLLAMA_MODELS=/shared/$USER/ollama-models
export OLLAMA_HOST=0.0.0.0:11434

if ps -eo cmd | grep -q "[b]in/ollama serve"; then
    echo "already running"; exit 0
fi
cd /shared/$USER
nohup setsid "$BIN" serve > "$LOG" 2>&1 < /dev/null &
echo "launched pid $!"
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{Pull the weights}

\begin{lstlisting}
gpu shell bash -lc 'export OLLAMA_MODELS=/shared/$USER/ollama-models; \
  /shared/$USER/opt/ollama/bin/ollama pull qwen3.5:122b-a10b'
\end{lstlisting}

Because \cmd{OLLAMA\_MODELS} points at \cmd{/shared}, the pull happens once and
is reused by every later claim --- and by teammates. Sustained download rate is
around 110--120\,MB/s, though the tail of a large blob has been observed to
collapse to roughly 1\,MB/s for the final gigabyte; the pull still completes.

\subsection{Capabilities are not interchangeable}
\label{sec:capabilities}

A model's declared capabilities decide what it can be used \textit{for}, and no
configuration can add a missing one. Query them before wiring a model into
anything:

\begin{lstlisting}
curl -s http://localhost:11434/api/show -d '{"model":"qwen3.5:122b-a10b"}' \
  | python3 -c "import json,sys; print(json.load(sys.stdin)['capabilities'])"
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{table}[!ht]
  \centering
  \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.25}
  \begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}X c c c c@{}}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Model} & \textbf{completion} & \textbf{tools} & \textbf{vision} & \textbf{thinking} \\
    \midrule
    \cmd{qwen3.5:122b-a10b} & yes & yes & yes & yes \\
    \cmd{gpt-oss:120b}      & yes & yes & \textbf{no}  & yes \\
    \cmd{bge-m3:latest}     & \multicolumn{4}{c}{\textit{embedding only}} \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabularx}
  \caption{Declared capabilities. Two consequences follow directly: a model
  without \textit{tools} cannot drive an agent, and a model without
  \textit{vision} cannot read an image.}
  \label{tab:gpu-capabilities}
\end{table}

An agent framework attaches a \cmd{tools} array to every request. Sending that
to a model lacking the capability is rejected outright:

\begin{lstlisting}
HTTP 400
{"error":{"message":"registry.ollama.ai/library/<model> does not support tools",
          "type":"invalid_request_error"}}
\end{lstlisting}

The same model answers normally when the array is absent, so the model is not
faulty --- it simply cannot participate in an agent loop.

\subsection{Measured behaviour}
\label{sec:measured}

Both chat models were put through an identical battery on the same endpoint:
the same prompts, the same figure, and the same scoring. Everything in
Table~\ref{tab:gpu-measured} is measured, not quoted from a model card.

\begin{table}[!ht]
  \centering
  \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.25}
  \begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}X r r@{}}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Quantity} & \cmd{gpt-oss:120b} & \cmd{qwen3.5:122b-a10b} \\
    \midrule
    Size on disk                  & 65.4\,GB  & 81.4\,GB \\
    VRAM resident                 & 67.0\,GB  & 84.9\,GB \\
    Context served                & 131\,072  & 262\,144 \\
    Cold load and first reply     & 43\,s     & 28.6\,s \\
    Generation, warm              & \textbf{183.5} tok/s & 126.8 tok/s \\
    Tokens to emit ``CONNECTION OK'' & \textbf{68} & 253 \\
    Tool call, first turn         & 0.8\,s    & 1.0\,s \\
    Edit call, second turn        & 1.1\,s    & 3.7\,s \\
    \cmd{old\_string} byte-exact  & yes       & yes \\
    Edited code correct           & yes       & yes \\
    Vision                        & \textit{not supported} & \textbf{yes} \\
    Figure $\rightarrow$ TikZ compiles & ---  & yes \\
    Figure $\rightarrow$ TikZ, SSIM    & ---  & 0.9441 \\
    Figure $\rightarrow$ TikZ, ink overlap & --- & 0.4281 \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabularx}
  \caption{Head-to-head on one RTX PRO 6000. Keep-alive on both is 5 minutes
  idle by configuration, though a longer effective value has been observed.}
  \label{tab:gpu-measured}
\end{table}

\paragraph{Reading the table.}
\cmd{gpt-oss:120b} generates about 45\,\% faster and is markedly less verbose ---
68 completion tokens to answer a two-word prompt against 253. In an agent loop
that difference compounds across every turn, in both latency and context
consumed.

\cmd{qwen3.5:122b-a10b} pays for that with vision and twice the context, and
matches \cmd{gpt-oss} exactly where it matters most for agent work: both
produced a byte-exact \cmd{old\_string} for a search-and-replace edit and both
yielded correct, compiling code. Byte-exactness is the usual failure mode for
weaker models --- a paraphrased original silently fails to apply.

On the figure-to-TikZ task its ink overlap of 0.4281 is more than four times
that of the dedicated vision model it replaced (\cmd{qwen2.5vl:32b}, 0.1019),
though still below the deterministic converter's 0.7015. The model's output is
semantic and editable; the converter's is a coordinate dump.

\paragraph{VRAM constrains the context window.}
With \cmd{qwen3.5:122b-a10b} resident, \cmd{nvidia-smi} reports 84\,952\,MiB of
97\,887 in use --- roughly 13\,GB free. The KV cache for a 256\,K context on a
122\,B model does not fit in that, so a client configured for the model's full
native context will fail on allocation during long conversations or large
retrievals. Configure clients for 131\,072 on a single card, or claim a second
card (Section~\ref{sec:borrowing}) before raising it.
