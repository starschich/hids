\section{DC Fault Ride-Through}
\label{sec:dc-fault}

A fault on the DC side of a VSC HVDC link interrupts the power transfer. On a
link whose DC circuit is at least partly an overhead line, most of these
faults are temporary flashovers --- lightning, pollution, vegetation --- and
the link is expected to clear the fault and restore transmission on its own,
as an AC line does by auto-reclosure. The term used throughout is the one of
project specifications: a \emph{DC line fault recovery sequence}, a defined
series of restart attempts after a fault on the DC line. The expression
\emph{DC fault ride-through} in the chapter title names the same behaviour
from the point of view of the AC network.

The chapter is in two parts. Sections~\ref{sec:dcf-faults}
to~\ref{sec:dcf-alternatives} state the principles for the converter type
most VSC links use, the half-bridge MMC in a symmetrical monopole: how the
two fault types behave, with the first milliseconds taken from the EMT
benchmark of CIGRE TB~832~[26], what a recovery sequence consists of, which
switchgear it operates, how the fault location is identified, what the
recoveries do to the ratings, and which topologies and devices would
shorten them. Section~\ref{sec:dcf-pugalur} then applies the principles to
one installation, the $\pm$320\,kV Pugalur--Thrissur link in India, whose
supplier described the design~[24] and whose operator evaluated five years
of fault records~[25]; the recorded voltages and currents of that section
are the evidence for the statements of the first part. Every number in the
chapter comes from one of these three sources.
