\section{Conclusions and recommendations}

\subsection{Conclusions}
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0.25em}
  \item Availability and performance guarantees --- measured per CIGRE TB~956 as
        FEU, SEU and FOR --- are the commercially decisive terms for an HVDC
        supply contract serving data-center load.
  \item Realistically guaranteeable values are FEU $\approx$0.5\,\%,
        SEU $\approx$1\,\% and a pole FOR of order 2-3/year; more aggressive
        figures are commercial, not technical, commitments.
  \item A single HVDC link cannot meet a Tier~III data-center target unaided, so
        the LD regime must be designed together with the redundancy and storage
        strategy, not after it.
  \item Typical LD ranges are (\$0.1--1.2\,M per additional outage, \$75--600\,k per FEU
        increment, 3--5\,\% cap) are the starting point, not the target.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Recommendations}
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{0.25em}
  \item Set LDs as a percentage of OEM supply value, sized to incentivize
        remediation.
  \item Anchor the LD basis to PPA unavailability charges and to battery
        replacement cost.
  \item Argue for an LD cap linked to the overall PPA liability rather than the
        typical 3--5\,\% of HVDC contract value..
  \item Adopt a 3-year (best-3-of-4) monitoring period.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Open action items}
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0.2em}
  \item Research the PPA unavailability penalties charged by the data center, to
        use as the LD basis.
  \item Model the battery-replacement cost as a concrete LD justification.
  \item Complete the overall availability model (HVDC $+$ batteries $+$ on-site
        generation).
\end{itemize}
